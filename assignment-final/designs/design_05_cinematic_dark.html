III.
Fourteen years on the clock

Both lines fell. They never crossed.

Across two decades of healthcare reform, recession, and pandemic, U.S. ED wait times dropped by more than half. The gap between women and men narrowed — and then plateaued. Year by year, pain score by pain score, the pattern persists.

In 2009, at the highest pain band, women waited +5.0 min longer than men with identical reports.

IV.
A country-wide pattern

The map agrees. Unevenly.

Public NHAMCS files only resolve four Census regions. The gap is present in all of them — its magnitude varies. The Midwest carries the heaviest weight, twice the national mean.

Red regions: women wait longer. Blue: men do. Drag the year. Press PLAY.

V.
Where it gets worse

Sex is not the only line being drawn. Race compounds it.

The single largest deviation in this dataset is not at peak pain. It is for NH Black women at mild pain — five minutes above the band median, at a pain level the system is least likely to take seriously.

The ER is not neutral.

It is the most precisely measured part of American medicine, and what it has been measuring — for fourteen years, across every region, every pain level, every triage acuity — is a difference that should not exist.
